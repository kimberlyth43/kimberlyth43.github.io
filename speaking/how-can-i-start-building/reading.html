How Can I Start Building and Fighting?

Nehemiah 1:1–2:8

Preached by Nathan Colestock — July 20, 2025

Christ the King Church · Stillwater, MN

July 20, 2025Neh 1:1-2:12Christ the King Church

BUILD BY FAITH

You know that our strategy is to build and fight. I hope by now you find it pretty exciting and inspirational. But perhaps you are still wondering, "How do I start building? What am I supposed to do when I see an opportunity to build?" Wouldn't it be helpful if we had an example that we could look at to say, "Imitate that!" Well, that's exactly what we have in Neh 1:1-2:10.

At the start of Nehemiah, it is the 20th year of the king. So, it's been about 13 years since Ezra-Nehemiah (based on the shorter chronology). completed in four years between 2nd and the 6th year. So, there should have been lots of progress made in Jerusalem. And there was some. In 9:9, Ezra thanks God for a wall in Judah and in Jerusalem. Some versions translate "protection" because they believe it's a metaphor. But since in Nehemiah 1:2 Hanani comes to Nehemiah with the bad news that the city is broken down and the gates are burned with fire, I think they made lots of progress and the enemies of God attacked and destroyed it. That's why the remnant is in trouble and shame.

Every good story needs a problem to solve, a mountain to climb, an enemy to defeat, obstacles to overcome. In Ezra, the story was all about getting the temple built to establish worship in the land. This story will be about getting the city/walls built so that God is honored in the land.

Since God has staked his glory and his word on his people, their trouble and shame reflects poorly on him. When they were sent to exile for judgement, their shame demonstrated God's justice and showed his glory. But now that they've returned and seek to serve God however imperfectly, their shame and trouble may make the nations wonder if God will keep his word to rebuild his people. After all, he said in Deuteronomy 4, "The LORD will scatter you among the people...but from there you will seek the LORD your God and you will find him...."

Nehemiah wants to see God's promise fulfilled. He wants to build and fight so that God's glory is clearly evident in the fortunes of his people. And the way he gets started is an example and an inspiration to us. Like Paul says, "Follow me as I follow Christ." We can follow Nehemiah as a good example of how to start building and fighting.

He remains loyal to God in exile.

He confesses his sins. He doesn't shift blame or feel better by comparison but leans in God's Word.

He trusts in God's Word.

He prays to ask for God's help.

He makes a detailed, wise plan.

He courageously acts.

STAY LOYAL

Let's look first at Nehemiah's example of staying loyal to God in exile. When Nehemiah receives word that the remnant is in trouble and shame, he mourns. Verse four says he mourned "for days."

This is how we can see Nehemiah's loyalty. He still really cared for the mission of God and the remnant of his people. He didn't adapt his values to his surroundings. He didn't become of Susa while he was in Susa. He maintains his loyalty to God's people. He aches for their setbacks. When God's people suffer shame, he doesn't make light of it. He's gotten to a high position of power, the cupbearer to the king. He could have eased into a cushy life and forgotten about the remnant. But he doesn't. His heart is motivated by God's glory, and he knows that Jerusalem should be rebuilt for God's glory. So he weeps when the project is in danger.

The first step to building and fighting is cultivating a deep, personal, unshakable loyalty to God. At first, this is not the work of the will but is ultimately only from God. He creates in you a new heart. He gives you a new spirit of regeneration, a heart of flesh. If Christ is not your king. If your heart is still stony and cold toward him and his ways, pray to the Father and ask him for repentance to turn from your unbelief and trust him and receive a heart soft to his voice, ready to obey, and cast down for its sin.

Even after God has given you a new heart, you must take care of it. “Keep your heart with all vigilance, / for from it flow the springs of life" (Prov 4:23).

Too many Christians have given in to the passions of our nation. Their instincts no longer want to fight for Christ. They abandon their brothers and sisters who defend God's unpopular truth. Their devotion is for typical American sensibilities. Instead of being salt and light for the world, they hide their light and lose their saltiness. You could call this "going native." They live in the land and become part of it instead of remaining a counter-culture. They lose their loyalty to the comfort of fitting in.

One of the things you might be tempted to be embarrassed by is how different your Christian life will look than our world's. The operating system for our nation's culture is entertainment. We try to gamify everything from education to religion. The result is a shallow devotion of a constant spectator.

This "God is my belly" approach to life trivializes everything it touches. In the 1980s or 90s Neil Postman wrote "Amusing Ourselves to Death." He saw even from a secular perspective that our over-emphasis on passive consumption for fun would ruin us. His point is that in America everything has become a performance. From politics to church services, everything is supposed to be engaging, which is a code word for entertaining.

Now, having fun and being engaged is great. But if we make it our god, if we indulge this appetite too much, we'll find our loyalties lie with either the most interesting screen at this moment, or with our favorite talking heads on that screen.

Nehemiah's first example to us is his tears. His tears show his loyalty. What disappoints you? Do your disappointments demonstrate your loyalty to God, or to another? If your building and fighting does not come from a heart loyal to God, it will be for nought. In fact, worse than that, you will be building up your own competing kingdom instead of contributing to God's. Test yourself, so that you are in the world, but not of the world.

TAKE RESPONSIBILITY

When Nehemiah opens his mouth, the first thing out is a confession. “Even I and my father's house have sinned." He doesn't pretend that he has not been part of the problem that led to this shameful outcome. He takes responsibility.

This is not a perfunctory way of approaching God. He spends days in prayer and fasting. The prayer recorded here is a summary of these days, and in fact months of praying before he goes into the king looking sad.

As a quick side note before I talk about the importance of confession and taking responsibility, I want to draw your attention to the days Nehemiah spends mourning and praying and fasting. Because we can usually get anything we want with next-day shipping, we've lost the pace of heart required to fast and pray. There's no drive-through window for spiritual life. God didn't make our hearts operate at the speed of micro-processors. He didn't even make them operate at the pace of our minds. That's why many of us can comprehend a situation hours or days or even weeks before it sets in and "hits us." To live wisely, to live whole-heartedly, you may need to create more space for reflection and prayer.

Often, this kind of reflection enables you to take responsibility and give a full confession instead of trying to deflect blame. "They must not be turning to God with their hearts." It would have been easy to compare, "I'm not as bad as themLord. They intermarried after all!"

Reformation starts with confession. When we recognize the problem is not just about those other people. The fault is not with God. We are part of the problem. I am part of the problem. Nehemiah knows that God's judgement is against his people because they have acted corrupted and ignores his statues and commandments (1:7). Remember, sin is actually a problem. It's not just an abstract entity out there somewhere in the cosmic legal world. Sin is a relational rift, a rebellion against God that makes a separation between unholy us and holy him. God hates sin. So the first step in building and fighting is dealing with sin.

As you prepare to build back up the ruins that seem destroyed, first acknowledge the role you have played in the problem.

If it's your marriage which has gotten rocky, start by confessing all the sins you can. Don't shift the blame to your spouse. Don't compare yourself to other husbands or wives to feel better about yourself. Take responsibility and confess your sins.

If your children are bucking against your authority, first confess any of your own sins, perhaps of anger or impatience. Don't misuse your parental authority to continually criticize them. Don't compare yourself to other parents and puff yourself up. Confess your sins.

And this principle holds true in big-time reformation and revival as well. If we want to see a change from trivial, half-hearted, entertainment-focused, compromised Christian worship all too common in our day, then we need to first acknowledge our part. We've also trivialized God. We've said nothing when we could have spoken up. We've been willfully ignorant because we were going along with the crowd.

TRUST GOD’S WORD

Notice though, as you confess, the hope of Nehemiah. In v. 5 Nehemiah reminds himself and includes in his prayer that God is a God who keeps covenant and steadfast love with those who keep his commandments. That might seem an odd thing to say for someone who is about to admit to acting corruptly and not keeping the commandments. What is God's steadfast love for those who keep his commandments going to do for commandment breakers?

Nehemiah has studied the scriptures and knows that there is still hope. God promised that when His people returned to Him, He would gather them from all the lands of exile and dwell with them again. At the beginning of the sermon I showed you this from Deuteronomy 4. Here it is again from Deuteronomy 30, after God lays out the blessings for obedience and curses for rebellion.

Read Deut 30:1-5

Nehemiah takes God at his word and trusts it with specificity. He doesn't pray, "Lord, maybe this return was not meant to be and we misunderstood the scriptures about when we'd return. Perhaps you will act now, and perhaps in another generation." He doesn't make God's promises abstract and general where they are concrete and specific.

Many have observed that it's easy to love humanity in the abstract. What's difficult is to love your sibling specifically. While love is general, it's easy to have a sentimental notion for "people." What is difficult and commanded by God is to love the specific people he places in your life in the circumstances he brings them there. Loving "generally" actually cheapens love. It gives you the notion that it will be all sunshine and rainbows and fun fun fun to love everyone.

In much the same way, faith in the abstract is easy. What is difficult is to trust God with specificity. This is why trials are so valuable for us. They make us trust God with specificity. When money is tight, you don't "trust God to provide." You pray like crazy for God to provide some odd jobs this month so your rent check won't bounce and with faith trust that he will. When a child goes wayward, you don't "trust God to take care of them." You beg him to bring them back to the faith and give them repentance.

Underneath all our lives after God has given us new hearts should be a sense of general loyalty, "I'll do anything you say, Lord." But, like Nehemiah, that general faith should get specific in two ways.

First, it should get specific with God's Word. Notice that Nehemiah quotes God's word back to him in v. 9. He trusts specific passages of God's word. Don't trust the word generally. Dive in and find promises and teachings and doctrine to build your life on. Stake your marriage on principles like, "Be slow to anger, quick to listen, slow to speak." Stake your children on teaching like, "He who spares the rod hates his son." Stake your business on passages like, "The way of the wicked will perish."

Second, it should get specific in what it trusts for. Nehemiah doesn't just trust the passage, he rightly interprets and wisely applies it. In v. 10 he says that the remnant are the servants and the people that God has promised to gather and dwell with. Nehemiah looks at God's Word, and then he trusts God to fulfill it in the world right there before his eyes.

So, Nehemiah had a passage and he trusted it in the real world, asking God to do something specific for the remnant in Jerusalem. What passage should we trust? Well, all of them. Trust all of them. If you are working to rebuild your personal discipline or holiness, or marriage, or parenting, or vocation, ransack God's word to trust it and pray specifically. As a church, as we work to build and fight, I hope you'll join me in trusting the Great Commission.

Our Lord said, "All authority on heaven and on earth has been given to me. Therefore, as you go, make disciples of all nations baptizing them and teaching them to obey all I have commanded you. And lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age."

Here Jesus gives us two promises and our marching orders. The marching orders are to make disciples by baptizing and teaching them. The promises are at the beginning and the end. Jesus has all authority and Jesus is with us always. Now, how can you trust this with specificity?

Mom's, sometime tomorrow you'll be inundated with requests from a few kids, you'll be trying to remember what you were going to write on the grocery list, thinking about how many places to set at the table for the people coming over that night, and wondering how so much rice is still stuck to the floor even after you tried to clean it up after dinner last night. You'll be tempted to impatience or despair. But this passage gives you everything you need. Being a mom means making disciples of your own children. You're serving your savior, the one who has all authority and has promised to be with you. So, you can trust that he is with you and helping you, respond with love for your children, teach them to count it all joy when you face trials by your example, and press ahead in obedience because you're glad Jesus has authority over you. You can trust that God will give you everything you need that afternoon to love your children, teach them to be disciples, and put dinner on the table for your guests.

Gentlemen, tomorrow you'll show up at work. Your email inbox will be full. You'll have a difficult problem you need to figure out. In the back of your mind you'll be hoping for a raise to relieve some financial strain and provide for your house. And you'll be trying to figure out how you can leave in time to drive your kids to their evening activity. You'll be tempted to anger or impatience or discontentment. But again, this passage gives you everything you need. To make disciples in all of life, someone needs to figure out and show the world what God honoring supply-chains, financial structures, loan packages, delivery services, restaurant care, and construction looks like. You're there to make disciples and teach them to obey all that God has commanded you. I hope you have chances to proclaim the gospel to your coworkers and employees. Even if those are few and far between, you are learning and teaching them to obey all Christ has commanded in honest business practices and a hard day's work. You're one of his ambassadors in your company. So you can trust him for that day. You can trust him to be present and give you the grace you need for your tough morning meeting. You can trust that the "all authority" Jesus has includes your boss when you're mistreated and continue on without bitterness.

As a church, we can trust that God wants us to be here. He's established us here in the Stillwater area. We can trust that he wants us to stick around, and so trust him for a permanent location. We can trust God to prepare a building for us to worship him in because we have seen great evidence that he wants us to be here and make disciples.

ASK FOR GOD’S HELP

Nehemiah not only trusts God’s word, he thinks to ask God for his help. He doesn’t think that he’s smart enough, strong enough, good enough to take care of this all on his own. He knows that he needs God’s help. So he asks. You can see him in v. 11 asking for God to be attentive to his prayer. In fact, you find out that he’s got a group of people he’s recruited to pray.

There are at least two reasons that we often skip prayer. The first is that we are too self-confident. We don’t think we need God’s help. The second is because, just like we talked about, we lack faith in the specifics to believe that God cares about us and listens to us.

Church, you believe that God hears your prayers and delights in you? That He cares about you specifically, not just His people generally. That He is orchestrating and intervening in your life for your good and for others. He does. And he proves it to us over and over. He proves it doctrinally, telling us He chose us from before the foundation of the world. He shows us poetically, assuring us that God knows details about us, so interested in us that He counts the hairs on our head. He says that birds do not fall to the ground without Him knowing. He clothes the grass of the field and considers us more valuable than His birds and plants. And he proves it specifically. You’re here, after all, aren’t you?

MAKE A PLAN

Nehemiah does not just go flying into the king by the seat of pants. He’s got a plan in mind. In fact, it’s really specific. He’s got a plan to leverage his station as the cupbearer and the talents that have made him successful there. He’s thought so much about it in the months between Hanani’s arrival and Nisan when he goes to the king that he’s got a departure and return date in mind. He knows what letters he would like. He’s ready to go and asking God to bless the plan.

He’s not praying, “Please do something, Lord.” He’s got a day, a plan, a materials list, everything ready that can be ready so that when God gets him a green light from the king he’s ready to go. Because Nehemiah has specific faith, he can make specific plans and trust God to bless them.

Notice how Nehemiah follows this pattern. When he hears that Jerusalem is in ruins, he has the holy desire to help the rebuilding. His plans leverage his station as cupbearer and the talents that have made him successful in that role. He’s got an open door to the king. He’s roped in others to pray and, presumably, to plan and aid him, providing him with wise counsel. He’s using biblical wisdom soaked in the promises of God from his word.

You can apply this to both where to build and fight (changing jobs, people to invest in, businesses to start, etc.) and in making plans for them to succeed.

Nehemiah didn't plan enough to say, "Well, God, you'll have to just make it work." Most of the time, God doesn't work like he did with Gideon's 300 men who supernaturally terrify the opposing armies and they kill themselves and flee. Most of the time God works through well-laid plans, like David storing up for the building of the temple. Or Ezra preparing for years before leading the people in the worship of God. Nehemiah planned enough to say, "Lord, I have done everything that I can think to do. And now, please prosper this plan." He made plans with a reasonable likelihood of success and asked God to bless them.

Laying plans like this is part of how I believe God will transform this area. It's by our brainstorming, and planning and praying and executing. I know some of you are planning to strategically move closer to the area. Some of you are planning business or career changes. Some of you are making plans to improve your marriage and parenting. And you should see in the example of Nehemiah that this godly planning is exactly what you should be doing. As you plan you are exercising biblical wisdom and working to take dominion. It's precisely what you should be doing.

The Bible says that man makes his plans, but the Lord establishes his steps. Amen. It also says that unless the Lord builds the house, the builders labor in vain. But that doesn't mean that the builders will show up at the jobsite and the house is done. It means that it requires God's blessing in their work and God's blessing in the planning and execution.

As you plan and choose which opportunities to take, especially take account of the station God has given you. Nehemiah was cupbearer to the king. That's not a role you end up in accidentally. At the time, it was common to try to kill the king by poisoning him. The cupbearer's job was to taste all of the food before it came to the king. And thus he was pretty motivated to make sure that the whole kitchen and supply line was free of evil actors. As one of the most trusted men in court, he likely would have been given quite a bit more authority and work because the king believes that he is working in his best interest.

(There's a biblical principle to note there, authority flows toward integrity and responsibility).

COURAGEOUSLY ACT

Finally, all this faith and prayer and planning wouldn't have accomplished anything if Nehemiah didn't have the courage to act. Nehemiah planned for the journey, he charted the course, but he wasn't going to get anywhere unless he had the courage to set sail.

And do not forget how important this last part is. Many will plan and pray, and when the time for ACTION and courageous action comes, they shrink back. Nehemiah was afraid (2:2). He took his life in his hands, risking awful death for appearing disloyal to the king. You might be wondering why he is so afraid. Well, the cupbearer was a very powerful role. Like I said, often lots of other authority came with it and it became a Joseph-like role. This request could easily be interpreted as setting up a place to launch from and challenging the king. It could look like he's trying to set up a rival capital. That's why the first thing he says is redeclaring his loyalty to the king. Then he goes on with his request.

So when he says he was very afraid, we can take him at his word. If the king thought he was a traitor, that was going to be it for him. He'd die in some awful way, likely tortured until God mercifully took his soul. It's a mark of his loyalty and faith that he has the courage to continue even in the face of such stiff consequences.

Nehemiahs show us that building and fighting with God includes accepting risk. As John Piper has famously said, risk is right. Trust God, make a good plan, ask him to bless it, and get going.

CONCLUSION AND EXAMPLES

So, Nehemiah is an example of how to get our building and fight off the ground.

He remains loyal to God in exile.

He confesses his sins. He doesn't shift blame or feel better by comparison but leans in God's Word.

He trusts in God's Word.

He prays to ask for God's help.

He makes a detailed, wise plan.

He courageously acts.

Whatever you are planning to build up next, follow God's wisdom set down by Nehemiah.

Perhaps you are trying to build a financially stable house, and you are not sure how to get out. Start by taking responsibility. Know that God will take care of you. He hears His children. Ask Him for wisdom and help you to increase patience and wisdom. Create a budget. Set spending goals. Pray about what God would bless in your plan, and then go to work and be thrifty.

If you are not happy with your marriage, start by taking responsibility. Confess your sins to God and to your spouse. Pray for God's help and start to make a plan. If your affection for your spouse is wavering, consider mentors. Search the Scriptures. If you know people doing well in this area, ask for their advice. And when you have a plan, you are pretty sure God will bless it. Ask God to bless it. And get to work.

If you have been stagnant in your work or career, plateauing for a while, take responsibility for where you have allowed yourself to settle. Does this honor your God, your family, or your boss? Search the Scriptures to appreciate current God's ideas of your work. Make a plan to get to a new level. Make a list of the accountabilities you are committing to. And then make it happen.

Maybe you look at the world's really big problems: increasing crime, foolish policies, and you want to see it change. Confess any part you have played in the problem. Consider where you are in life, or what you would have to do to get to a position where you could make a tangible impact. Pray for God's blessing, and build a plan.

I hope that Nehemiah's example and those six pieces of it make building and fighting less conceptual and more concrete. All you need is...

A ruin to rebuild

A heart to obey

A humble spirit

Faith in God's word

Time to pray

A plan to follow

And the courage to start.

And as we go, we know that our tiny efforts aren't decisive. They're blessed by God as we're swept up in the rushing tide of his plan to make sure Christ is preeminent in all things.

Amen.
