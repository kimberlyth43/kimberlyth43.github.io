Introduction

In my introductory sermon a couple weeks ago we saw that the story of Ezra Nehemiah is much like our own. God called them out of exile in Persia to build his temple and city. It will end with all things made new, the church as a spotless bride, the city of God expanding as the increase of Christ's government has no end, and even death swallowed up in victory.

This week our text tells us the outset of the story. And most basically what we see is God calling his people to return to the land and rebuild the temple and city. 2. Give thanks to God for how he stirs up pagan rulers to do good.

3. Understand what it means to rebuild the temple and the city.

4. Receive our mission and role from God.

3. Understand What It Means to Rebuild the Temple and the City

Next, we're going to consider what it means to rebuild the temple and the city. We learned from Daniel that the rebuilding will happen in two phases. The rebuilding that the people of Ezra-Nehemiah was phase one. And our building God's temple and city (the church) is phase two.

In their day, the temple was destroyed by Babylon attacked Jerusalem. So the temple structure needs to be rebuilt. But it's not just a mission to rebuild the temple. For the temple to function the way that it should, a whole society needs to surround it. The temple needs the city of of Jerusalem rebuilt so that the the priests and levites serving the temple have a place to live. And the city needs to be populated by more than the priests and levites so that there is enough economic activity to supply bread and meat clothing and everything else to live.

And the surrounding towns of Jerusalem need to be populated so that there are people to come and worship God including giving a tithe to the levites so that the levites have what they need to live and serve in the temple and teach the people. Building the temple means building a whole society. The mission is to restore the worship of God which includes rebuilding the society that supports the worship of God.

In our chapter the story, we are here to rebuild the temple and the city as well. And we've seen from scripture that both of these are the church. Not the local church, the universal church (which we confess we believe in in the Nicene Creed). The universal church is the society of all believers. And the universal church is manifest in local churches all over the world. But let's make a careful distinction here lest we accidentally make a secular sacred divide.

Our task in building the city of God and the temple is not just church planting. Church planting is crucial. We need healthy churches as we keep building up the body of Christ. But local churches are just one peice in the society we are looking to build.

Local churches have an important role to play in building the city of God. But they aren't all that needs to be built. The job of local churches is just what our mission statement says. Churches are here to make disciples--the kind of disciples who glorify God in all of life. Churches do that by ministering the Word of God and the sacraments or ordanices of God (baptism and the Lord's Supper).

But local churches aren't here to run schools, protect the public from criminals, make new inventions, sling software code, produce food, repair snowmobiles, run resteraunts, or build decks. And yet all of life is supposed to be transformed to the glory of God. The city of God isn't limited to Sunday mornings. That's where all of us members come in. The local church's mission is to make disciples who glorify God in all of life. And each Christian's mission is to glorify God in all of life! As Jesus builds his church, as the temple is constructed and city walls erected, it knows no boundaries.

The gathered church as an institution with elders and deacons (which is just one aspect of what we are called to build) has a particular job given to it by God. It's focused on making disciples and teaching them to obey all that Jesus commanded. Just like government has the particular job of enforcing justice and the family of rearing children. The scattered church as an organic society that spreads across the whole community is to infiltrate every area of life to reclaim it for God's glory. We should plant churches to be launch pads and supports to Christians building and fighting everywhere. God not only calls us together to worship each week. He commissions us out each week to serve him in the world.

So what are we building? A society, a community, a civilization with art and culture and skills and churches and businesses and schools and households and charities and crisis pregnancy centers and tutors and charities and missions agencies and production studios and marketing agencies and engineering firms. We're building a full-orbed culture with food and dancing and holidays and traditions. This culture will start like a mustard seed, small in an insignificant corner where no one notices. And God willing it will grow.

And since our sacred mission encompasses all of life, every roll is is sacred. No one's job is more sacred than another. God has called me to full time Christian service to him as a pastor. And God has called Brad to full time Christian service in defense sales and Nick to full time Christian service in constuction and Grant to full time Christian service in software and Lauren to full time Christian service as a homemaker. All of it is absolutely sacred. Everyone is called to full time Christian work building the city of God whether they're a mom, a teacher, a salesman, a business owner, a CEO, a hairstylist, a carpenter, a janitor, a student, or a toddler. Do you hear that kids. You toddle and play to God's glory. You've got an important role--what kind of a city would it be without children to laugh and boys to play ball and girls to do whatever young girls do together.

You probably noticed that the geneology from chapter two is pretty long. There's something to learn there. The work of rebuilding was not the work of a few elite believers. It was the work of hundreds of households. It changed their family tragectory from generations as they moved across the known world back to their homeland leaving everything these people had known behind.

And notice that they didn't all come to do the same thing. The geneology is in a few different lists, broken out by the basic role each will play in the work of reformation.

  • First is the leaders in v. 2
  • Then the people of Israel. The average guys.
  • v. 36 comes the priests
  • v. 40 the levites
  • v. 55 Solomon's servants
  • v. 58 the Temple servants

Notice that none of these roles is the sacred really important work while other stuff is the "has to be done" work. To accomplish the mission, all these people had different jobs. Some were keeping sheep while others cut those sheep up as sacrifices. Some grew crops, some cooked the food, some taught the people the law, some mened and wove new clothing, some beat iron into tools and weapons. They all had a different role toward the same mission. And no one's role was more sacred than another.

So for us. The work of reformation is not the work of a few super-christians. It's not mostlyh the work of your pastors. In fact, Ephesaisn tells us that it is the job of pastors to equip saints for the work of the ministry. All of life is fundamentally sacred, which means all honest work can be done to God's glory.

But that doesn't mean all work is equally effective. Jesus says, "By this my father is glorified: that you bear much fruit." Not all work produces the same amount of fruit. I'm not talking about on profession or another. And I'm certainly not saying that pastors produce the most fruit.

Here's what I mean. If you are working in the standard secular workplace, bogged down by beurocracy so that your work is nothing to you but a paycheck, look for other work. It's a good thing to provide well for your family, but you don't want dollar signs and commas, as important as they are, to be the only outcome of your work. You want to work in something that is personally meaningful to you as a Christian. And that doesn't mean that it all has to be missions work. Let me give you an example to make this concrete.

My dad has run his own business for a long time now. I'm privledged to get to work a little bit in it on the side. His company produces software that enables their client to manage a large fleet of power-sports vehicles and lease them to small to medium sized businesses that rent them to customers. My dad is a committed Christian, and he runs his company like one. He does his work with integrity and excellence. He puts the clients needs before his own. He's consistantly humble, helpful, and great to work for. He takes care of those who contract through him. He's a thoughtful leader who doesn't demand that everything go his way neither does he abdicate his responsibility to lead. He develops those who work for him so that they gain meaningful skill and experience and if they leave, they leave better than when they came. Over a dozen households find their main source of income as a direct result of working for him. In addition, his software enables his client to do business, helping provide for many dozens of jobs in their corporation not to speak of the hundreds of businesses and thousands of employees that use the software to run their business. That is creative, all-of-life encompassing city of God building. It's a household with Jesus' flag over expanding its influence into the dark world to shine the light of Jesus.

If someone said to him, "You should really direct your efforts to where it counts most. If you have a burden to build the kingdom, then receive your calling from God and do it with all your heart. You're called to those kids and that house and your husband. Receive your calling from God and do it with all your might.

If you find yourself in work thinking, "If you pulled me out of the company, I don't think much of anything would change in the real world." You should look for something different. And you've already been feeling that--wanting more meaning in your work. That's not because your field is unimportant. It could be because the company is structured poorly or because of team dynamics you aren't able to make much of a contribution or you just aren't well suited for the role you are in. That's fine. Go find someplace to bear much fruit.

In 1962 JFK visited NASA for the first time. During his visit he asked a janitor walking the hallway with a broom, "What do you do here?" The janitor replied, "I'm putting a man on the moon, sir." He knew his mission and how his part fit.

If I found you tomorrow morning as you start another work week and asked, "What are you doing here?" What would you say? I hope you'll say, "Building the city of God." Building the city of God one diaper change at a time, one email response at a time, one article written at a time. Men that's what your household is--it's a little unit of God's big city. How much can you expand yours? How much can you take dominion over for God? Especially for the next four years. This is the time to build like mad while you have the chance. The current policical plimate gives you the air cover you need to build build build until the work is put to a stop again. If you work in HR, now's a great time to get rid of those bad DEI policies. Now's the time to make money and start a side hustle. Position yourself for the next phase of the battle. Go for the promotion. Start the company. Whatever opportunities you have to take dominion--do it! That's what God made you for.

They built God's city with brick and mortor. We build it out of family dinners and ruckus singing and healthy churches and profitable businesses and clean kitchens and just cities. We build it by doing whatever we do as unto the Lord and not for man.

The people hear Cyrus' decree and they respond because God stirs in their heart too. Do you se that in v. 5. God stirs up the heart of his people to follow the decree of this pagan king whom he stirred in to make the decree.

Our call to rebuild comes in much the same way. God stirs in our hearts. He sovereignly calls us out of exile by his long-fortold decree, setting our hearts to do his will.

Stop there just a second to consider the power of God over you. God controls your will. What you want--what you feel like doing and not doing. God's in charge of that. And when he wants to, he changes it. That's why it makes sense to pray things like, "Change their mind, soften their heart." God does that. We don't have the control we might think we have. Our mind and hearts are not some kind of sanctuary that God refuses to enter. He's the Lord of everything, so he comes in to command us to do what he wants us to, whether or not we're aware of it. Humble yourself before the God who holds your heart in his hands, directing it where he will.

I don't think Cyrus or these people had some kind of really emotional experience and that's what "stir in their heart" meant. I think it's a way of saying that God set the direction of their desires.

So pray that God would change yhour heart. Are you envious? Self-righteous? Prideful? Fearful? Pray for God to help you. In my box of prayer cards at home I have one from 2021 asking God to help me love two people that were hard for me to love. And by God's grace, he's helped me as I keep asking him. Especially pray that God would give you a heart wholly devoted to the mission he has called you to.
