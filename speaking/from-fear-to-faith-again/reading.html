Preached by Nathan Colestock — March 23, 2025

Christ the King Church · Stillwater, MN

FROM FEAR TO FAITH AGAIN: A SAINT’S JOURNEY

EZRA 3–5

CHRIST THE KING CHURCH

MARCH 23, 2025

INTRODUCTION

These three chapters from Ezra tell a story all of us have lived: from fear to faith back to faith again. It’s a familiar story in Scripture:

Aaron joins Moses as God’s faithful mouthpiece to destroy Israel by the power of God in the ten plagues. But when Moses is gone up the mountain with God, he is afraid of the people and makes a golden calf for them. Later, he returns to faith and serves God.

Peter confesses Jesus is the Christ, and just a short while later denies him three times for fear of high-ranking Jews. When Jesus rises again, he believes and insists with Jesus that he loves him.

In this story, Israel starts off full of faith. They build the altar first, they make sacrifices, they celebrate the Feast of Booths, they even stand against the initial attack of the enemy. But when the opposition grows really stiff, they give in. God restores them to faith through the word of his prophets, and they pick up their God-appointed task once more.

Today we won’t quite finish the story, which ends in chapter 6 (spoiler alert, God defeats their enemies). By the end God has once again chosen what is foolish and weak and lowly and despised in the world, shamed the self-reliance of Man, and made sure no one can boast in presence. In this part of the story, we watch God’s people…

Run to God in faith while they are afraid of their enemies (ch. 3)

Fall from faith to lay low when the attack ramps up (ch. 4)

Repent of their fear and come back to faith in response to God’s Word (ch. 5)

Perhaps fear has pulled you off the straight and narrow path. Your fear of rejection or ridicule have convinced you to hunker down and stay out of God’s fight. Or your fear of failure or the pull to comfort made sure you stay out of God’s construction zone. This morning is an opportunity to return to the fear of the Lord.

The point of this story, which is also our outline, is this: 1) Seek first the Kingdom of God 2) lest fear of man and worldly desire choke out your faith. 3) When you sin, repent and fear God rather than men. This week I’ve been asking God to reveal our fear of man so that we can repent of it together and come back to him.

SEEK FIRST THE KINGDOM OF GOD

The 42,000 returnees get to the land and settle in their towns (2:70). Just a verse later they leave those new settlements and gather all together in the ruins of Jerusalem. They come together as one man (3:1) to kick off their grand mission to re-establish worship in the land of promise. They know worship is the reason they’ve returned. They are here to rebuild the temple so that God is worshiped again according to his statutes and rules. We know that God has promised his people many blessings: safe cities, fruitful vineyards, peaceful land, a just society. But working for those blessings isn’t what they do first. “Seek ye first the kingdom of God, and all these things will be added unto you.” They build the altar first.

Establishing right worship is always step one for God’s people. Maintaining right worship is always the highest priority for God’s people. They built an altar because in their time, God asked for animals. We learn to read music because in our time he asks us to sing Psalms (most of which we don’t know). The principle is the same. The highest priority of God’s people must be establishing right worship.

Now, worship is a broad term which can (and should) include everything we do in life. All of life is service to God, our spiritual act of worship. But that was true for Israel too. God has always demanded all-of-life service from his people. In addition to serving God in every aspect of life, God tells us how he wants to be worshiped here on earth in space and time. He told his people Israel he wanted to be worshiped by the levitical system including Sabbaths, festivals, and the temple sacrifices. So later in this chapter they begin constructing the temple. He’s told us he wants the saints to gather together regularly, and not forsake this gathering. When we gather, he’s told us to preach the word, sing the word (especially psalms), read the word, and proclaim his word in baptism and the Lord’s supper. And that’s what we’ve established. It’s purposeful that we haven’t started with a bunch of other programs. Worship is what we need to get right.

And “getting it right,” establishing right worship, isn’t something that you set up and it just keeps going. It’s not like a machine that just keeps running. It’s like a plant that needs constant watering and weeding. We must become people who love God with all our heart, soul, mind, and strength. We could train a group of atheists to walk through these motions. We could even train them to do it technically better. But just as God was not pleased merely by the blood of bulls and goats, he is not pleased merely by singing and preaching.

It will help here to observe the heartfelt worship of Israel when they gather together to make sacrifices. Because we are so removed from the Mosaic system of worship, and have been trained by generations of bad theology to think of the Old Testament worship as rote and unfeeling, we miss what’s going on here as Israel offers sacrifices and celebrates the Feast of Booths.

Each sacrifice was an act of faith performed because the worshiper wanted to draw close to God. They would bring an animal to God as a food offering, because they wanted to eat with God. They wanted to get to sit at his table. Oftentimes the priest would give a portion of the animal to God for altar’s fire to consume and the priest would eat a portion on behalf of the worshiper as their mediator. But for some sacrifices, including the free will offerings our text specifically says they made here, the people would eat the sacrifice themselves as well (Lev 7:16). They bring these sacrifices because they want to eat with God. This isn’t rote or mindless. It’s rich biblical faith. If you’d like to learn more about these sacrifices, you could check out chapter 2 of “A House for My Name” by Peter Leithart.

In this passage they also celebrate the Feast of Booths, also called the Feast of Ingathering. It came after the harvest where they saw God provide for them visible and tangibly. To the time of year, the Feast adds a rich layer of historical symbolism. For the eight days of the feast, the people would make sacrifices (eating with God all week long) and live in tents or booths to remind them of the forty years of Wilderness Wandering. To us living in a tent sounds like poverty. But biblically the imagery of a tent is of provision and protection such as in Psalm 27:5, “he will conceal me under the cover of his tent.” They are remembering how God protected and provided for them all those years in the wilderness when they lived forty without planting crops or hunting game because God provided food and water for them.

Just think about how meaningful this must have been to celebrate at this moment. They are back in the land, celebrating how God protected and provided for his people despite their sin and failing and faithlessness. They know they and their fathers have sinned, and that’s why they were in exile in the first place. But now God has graciously called them back after preserving them alive for seventy years.

We need to emulate the faith of Israel who wanted to meet with God, to sit at his table, to celebrate and remember what he’s already done for them and take hope knowing that he was still with them. Brothers and sisters, we need to learn to come hungry to meet with God. That’s what is happening here. As we gather we ascend with our voices into the heavenly places where we are seated with Christ. We need to learn to live lives of faith that are filled with longing and anticipation to be with God-not when we die. Now! As we gather each week to meet him in his word and at his table.

When we come together, God is with us and speaking to us. Establishing right worship for us meant first creating a worship service modeled after God’s commands. But that’s not it. Now we need to learn to come to God and worship like he asks. We’ve focused on singing because it feels most new. For us, living generations into abandoning the psalms, it takes some work to learn new tunes and sing our parts. Obedience for us has required lots of learning. But remember why you’re doing it. God has invited you to come into his presence and sing psalms because you are filled with the joy of being his child.

Consider now some of the other lifestyle changes that worshiping God rightly requires.

We read the Bible while we’re here because we want to receive God’s words. When we do, we don’t just read a verse here or there. We read a chapter or two or three at a time. It’s not a time in the service to just listen, or even just read along. It’s time to make a home in your heart for God’s Word. To receive it with thanksgiving, thankful that God is still speaking his living active word to you. In the midst of a culture rapidly losing the capacity to focus on anything longer than a TikTok video, it will take focused effort to be a person who comes to worship in command of your own attention so that you can hear God’s Word.

We get down on our knees and confess our sins. And though we don’t just spend a few seconds on our knees, it’s not enough time to gain a contrite heart if you haven’t walked humbly confessing your sins to God all week long. We need to develop a pattern of confession and a humble and contrite heart rather than giving into our therapeutic age.

We sit down together at the Lord’s Table every week. It’s the main reason we’re here, the climax of our service. But you won’t be able to come recently and joyfully to the table of the Lord if you haven’t meditated on his death at all the previous week.

Right now, our first priority should be learning to worship God rightly together. That means, learning to live in control of our attention so we can receive God’s word, learning to sing so we can sing Psalms, learning to confess our sin with a contrite heart before God, learning to meditate on the wondrous cross. Parents, this is how we need to teach our kids to come to church. It’s about more than sitting quietly for the sermon. God designed the Sabbath and Feasts to orient the life of Israel, to ground it to a life hungry to be God’s people and have him as their God. In the same way, God designed the Lord’s Day or orient our lives, and ground us to a life as God’s people.

Establishing right worship being our first priority does not mean we neglect the rest of life. In fact, all of life will wilt away if worship is not our first priority. Without the gravity of weekly worship to hold us together, our lives will fly into chaos. This worship service fills our hearts to go out singing in a dismal world. It fills our mind with truth to combat the spirit of the page. This is where we find rest for our souls so that we steady as all the world crumbles around us. One thing is needful. Seek first the kingdom of God, and all these things will be added unto you.

It would be reasonable to imagine that Israel’s gathering was filled with joy that they’ve made it back to the land. But our text tells us that a key part of their motivation in re-establishing worship so quickly is that they feared the peoples of the land (3:3). They gather in tents remembering God’s provision and protection begging God to keep up the protection because they are scared.

Now, coming to God because they were afraid and wanted protection does not make their displays of faith less genuine. They were doing exactly what they should. Instead of sending envoys to Egypt to protect them, or cry out to the gods of the land, or make peace treaties with the people of the land, they were gathered to ask for God’s help. God wants us to wait on him when we are afraid. He doesn’t penalize it as fake faith.

Church, when you are afraid, put your trust in him. Don’t run from your fears, don’t keep your music blasting or the TV playing so that you don’t have to think on them. Don’t try to amuse them away. Pray about them. And come to church with them so that you are encouraged that in your trials and hardships God is with you. Come with the faith of the Psalmist in Psalm 123:2,

“Behold, as the eyes of servants

look to the hand of their master,

as the eyes of a maidservent

to the hand of her mistress

so our eyes look to the LORD over God

till he has mercy on us.”

After building the altar and celebrating the feast, they make arrangements to begin building the temple. They sacrificially give toward the effort even though they know they may need their resources to defend themselves from their enemies because they are sure God will provide for them.

As they build the temple, we can see they are reading their story as the next installment in the cycle because they model their actions off what happened in the last cycle. They begin building the temple in the same month that Solomon began the construction of the first. They even sing the very same song when the foundation is complete.

“For he is good,

for his steadfast love endures forever toward Israel.”

But in the midst of this joyful celebration, a sorrowful wail breaks in. Verse twelve says that those who had seen the first temple raise a cry, presumably because they can’t imagine how the glory of the previous temple will ever be restored.

Into their hopelessness, God speaks a word of hope from Haggai and Zechariah. Ezra 5:1 tells us this is the time of their ministry, and their messages helps us understand this story better.

In Zechariah 4:8–10 God says,

“The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundations of this house; his hands shall also complete it…For whoever has despised the day of small things shall rejoice, and shall see the plumb line in the hand of Zerubabbel.”

God is not chastising them for their tears. He’s promising to turn their frowns upside-down. “Don’t worry!” he says, “You will see this temple with your own eyes and you will rejoice then.”

From Haggai 2:5–9 God adds,

“My Spirit remains in your midst. Fear not. For thus says the LORD of hosts: Yet once more, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth and the sea and the dry land. And I will shake all nations, so that the treasures of all the nations shall come in, and I will fill this house with glory, says the LORD of hosts. The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, declares the LORD of hosts. The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former, says the LORD of hosts. And in this place I will give peace, declares the LORD of hosts.”

We don’t know when Zechariah gave his prophecy, but we know that Haggai’s prophecy came on the last day of the Feast of Booths while all Israel was gathered. This was God’s message of hope to his people. He says, “I am with you, so you do not have to be afraid. Soon, I will shake the heavens and earth and the nations and take their treasures to deck my temple so that it’s more glorious than it ever has been.”

If only those old men could see the temple now. She’s not done, but God’s new temple, built of living stones, is simply stunning. Isn’t it? It’s spread out over all the earth. Century after century invading nation after nation spreading the great news of Christ’s once for all sacrifice that cleanses us so we can feast at God’s table each week. All around the world people from every nation, tribe, and tongue worship our God, the architect of our salvation.

If you’re tempted to despise our days of small beginnings here, if you thought there’d be more people, or it would feel more grand, or if you simply look at the scope of transforming the Stillwater area so that God’s will is done here as it is in heaven and think, “How could it ever be that way?” Look to all God has already done. We have every reason to hope. Listen to God’s Word through Haggai, “My Spirit remains in your midst. Fear not.”

LEST THE FEAR OF MAN AND WORLDLY DESIRE CHOKE YOUR FAITH

Now we come to the part of the story when the opposition begins. Israel will allow their fear of man and worldly desire to choke their faith. Let’s learn from their example so that we do not follow in their footsteps. Seek first the kingdom of God lest the fear of man and worldly desire choke your faith.

The enemy tries two tactics to disrupt their work. First, they try to join. “Let us come build with you” they say. These people are compromising “worshipers.” Maybe they’ve worshiped Yahweh, but it was in addition to their other pantheon of gods, not as the one true God. So, Israel’s leaders respond with a resounding, “No way, you have no share with us in building this temple.” At first their faith holds strong. That’s exactly the right response.

We may face the same kind of challenge: opposition masquerading as help. We must beware wolves that come in sheep’s clothing. They will come looking like Christians, and talking like Christians, but they really have no part in our building. As a new church, we especially need to beware malcontents. There are some people who hear about a new church in town and want to join because they have something against their current church (and the other dozen churches they’ve left in a huff). They don’t have a part in our worship, they just love a quarrel.

The way to guard against this play of the enemy is to follow Paul’s command to be of one mind (Phil 2:2). Notice that all of their leaders, Zerubabbel and all the heads of father’s houses, are united in their answer. Imagine how chaotic it would have been for Zerubabbel to say, “We’ll take all the help we can get” while the heads of father’s houses told them to pound sand. Their united mind protected Israel.

Your pastors regularly marvel and give thanks to God for how he has made us of one mind. And we should all be thankful for strong defense against the enemy.

When their efforts at sabotage by joining the effort don’t work, they turn to lawfare. They bribe counselors. They get the lawyers involved and by their political maneuvering steal Israel’s courage. When a new king comes to power, they write a letter back to him to try and stop their work. The letter is recorded in chapter four.

In it, They inform the king about the work of Israel to rebuild the city. They brand Israel politically radical, declaring that they will turn against the king of they complete their city. They bring half truths as evidence of their accusation, “Check the records and you’ll find they were a rebellious city.” Well, they wouldn’t roll over for Nebuchadnezzar (which is what the king will find now) but that certainly is no proof that they are planning a coup now. Finally, they close their letter with a bit of flattery, “Because we eat the salt of the palace and it is not fitting for us to witness the king’s dishonor, therefore we send and inform the king.”

Does this strategy sound familiar? Lawfare, slander, flattery, political maneuvering…this is the strategy of the enemies of God today. They brand faithful Christian engagement in culture as “facism.” They try to make us out as extremists. They bring armies of lawyers and books of regulations as their weapons.

Now, this strategy of lawfare is effective on two levels (in their day and ours).

It stops the work. The temple and city construction comes to a halt. The cakes stop being baked. The wedding photos aren’t taken. The Christian businesses stalled.

It leaves the people susceptible to the temptation to stop building and fighting for God, and to just wait it out. Idle hands become the devil’s workshop, and soon they feel pretty comfy and that temple-building was hard work.

This second effect proves to be the downfall of Israel in this story. We get a God’s-eye-view of the event in Haggai 1 when God says,

“Is it time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins? Consider your ways! Go up to the hills and bring wood and build the house, that I make take pleasure in it and that I may be glorified, says the LORD.”

Israel was using the political climate as an excuse to leave off building the house. “Let’s wait until things cool down.” Maybe they even said, “Let’s wait for ‘God’s timing.’” They wanted to build without danger, to fight without risk. But that’s not how serving God works. “In this world you will have trouble. Take heart. For I have overcome the world.” “Through many persecutions we must enter the Kingdom of God.” “All those who desire to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted.”

God forbid we leave off serving because there are risks involved. Count the cost. Take up your cross. Put your hand to the plow and don’t turn back.

Satan will continually tempt us to wait. He doesn’t need to convince us to never build again. He just needs to keep convincing us that now isn’t the right time. Don’t fall for that trap. Don’t wait forever to share the gospel because you’re afraid of the reception you will have. Don’t stay quiet forever because you’re afraid of the respect you will lose. Don’t shut up forever because you’re afraid of causing a scene. If you wait for fear of repercussions you will wait forever. As you wait, you become, day by day, a hearer of the word rather than a doer of the word as Israel did here.

WHEN YOU SIN, REPENT AND FEAR GOD RATHER THEN MEN

When Haggai declares to Israel their sin, they repent. They start building again. They obey. And the reason for their obedience is in Haggai 1:12, “The people feared the LORD.” Seek first the kingdom of God lest the fear of man and worldly desire choke your faith. When you sin, repent and fear God rather than men.

Now, Israel had really good reason to fear God. God reveals to them in Haggai 1 that because of their sin God has struck them with a drought and cursed all their labors. That’s why they are starving and thirsty and dressed in tatters. God says,

“You have sown much, and harvested little. You eat, but you never have enough; you drink, but you never have your fill. You clothe yourselves, but no one is warm. And he who earns wages does so to put them into a bag with holes…You looked for much, and behold, it came to little. And when you brought it home, I blew it away.”

Do you see what God is doing here? God proves to them that they are in more danger while they keep their enemies happy by not building because God is angry at them.

Step one of the fear of God is believe, deep in your bones, that the only safe place there is is under the smile of God. So no matter how perilous, obedience is worth it. Jesus teaches us, “Do not fear the one who can harm the body, fear the one who can throw both the soul and the body in hell.” Our fear of God should graduate to reverence, but it must at least start here.

If you have given in to the fear of man, so that you mostly stay quiet when God’s Word and the gospel would offend someone, or contradict their lifestyle. If you accommodate the wickedness of the world rather than exposing it as darkness. If you know what God is calling you to, but you keep making excuses about why now isn’t the right time and you’ll get to it later…STOP. Listen to God’s warning, “The way of the LORD is a stronghold to the blameless, but the destruction to evildoers” (Prov 10:29). You are not safe while you stubbornly refuse to God the way that God has shown you. Your greatest risk is the discipline of God for your stiff-neck, not whatever danger or inconvenience you perceive in the way of God. Turn back now. Today is the day of repentance. So stop waiting. Here’s a some questions to prompt your reflection and confession:

Do you have an extended family member that you’ve avoided sharing the gospel with all these years?

Do you have a co-worker who supports wickedness to your face, but you’ve never confronted them?

Have you resisted disciplining your children like God commands because your afraid of their reaction?

Have you settled in your work instead of taking up godly ambition to provide more for your family and love your neighbor because you’re afraid of failure or hard work?

Are you aware of bad habits or habitual sins but haven’t squashed them because you’re afraid the way of righteousness isn’t actually satisfying?

Do you know your entertainment choices need to change but you’ve grown comfortable and don’t want to?

Do you know you need to get your family’s spending under control, but still haven’t made a budget because you’re afraid of how your wife will react?

Have you fallen into a pattern of spending most evenings alone after the kids are in bed and your marriage is suffering, but neither of you will give up your “alone time”?

There is no danger so great or comfort so precious it’s not worth giving up to follow God. “I count everything as rubbish for the surpassing joy of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord.” Turn from your sin. God has promised to forgive your sins in Christ Jesus, and cleanse you from all unrighteousness (thanks be to God).

When we repent, we get the great joy of walking in the fear of the Lord. And he gives us the greatest gift we could have. It’s the message that puts wind in the sails of Israel’s faith so that they keep building even with opposition. It’s from Haggai 1:13, “I am with you declares the LORD.”

When they start building again, the local officials come by to see what’s going on. This shut them down before. What will they do this time? This time they even take names so that they can track down those responsible later. But Israel stands strong in faith and the eyes of God are on them so they didn’t stop while this second letter was sent.

Would you rather be safe in your paneled house, outwardly comfortable but with your conscience gnawing at your soul and without the approval of God, or sweaty working on the temple, looking behind your back for the enemy, but with the sure message that God is with you?

Friends, God has promised to be our God and that we would be his people. There is no danger, no loss, no risk that’s not worth having him for our God. If God is for us, who can be against us. Our hearts great desire, so strong it is restless until it finds it, is to know our God. To come back to his garden to walk in his pleasure. And he’s offered that us. He’s made a way into his good graces through the cross so that those who believe on his son are permanently forgiven and receive his Spirit to help them walk in his ways. That’s what your soul is hungry for. So turn from your sin and come to the table now, to eat and drink of his goodness, to receive his tangible grace, to renew covenant with our God who has promised to never leave us or forsake us.
