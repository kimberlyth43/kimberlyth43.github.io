Build and Fight

In our series on Ezra–Nehemiah we have come to a section that is especially important to our church. Nehemiah 4 is the inspiration for our strategy: build and fight. So this morning we want to make sure we all understand how to do that. Let’s begin by reminding ourselves how we fit into the story of Ezra–Nehemiah. Ezra–Nehemiah is part of a repeating pattern in Scripture where…

  • God gives his gracious decree.
  • God provides for his people and protects them.
  • God’s enemies attack his people.
  • God fights for his people, and God’s people conquer their enemies.
  • God’s people respond by consecrating themselves to him.
  • God greatly blesses them.
  • God’s people forget God and turn from him.

That has been the story over and over again—from Abraham and Jacob, through the Exodus and Joshua. Over and over God provides for and blesses his people, fights for them when they are attacked, and then, in thankfulness for the victory, his people consecrate themselves to him—only to forget him again. The old covenant did not transform their hearts, so they wandered.

We see this clearly in the two great projects of Ezra and Nehemiah: the temple and the walls. Both projects are completed by the grace of God, yet neither accomplishes what it should. The temple structure is finished, but it does not bring spiritual renewal. The walls stand tall and sturdy, but they do not keep sin and wickedness out. Israel does not become a city on a hill. Instead they invite the pagans in. This is not true reconciliation with God or reformation among the people.

But we live in the age of the new covenant, which brings new life to God’s people and opens a new chapter in the story. In our age God is building a new temple and a new city. They went to Jerusalem to establish the worship of God and to live as his holy people. They left Babylon, but God still had to take Babylon out of his people. Now he has. God has established a new temple, and 1 Peter tells us that we are the living stones of that temple. He filled this temple when fire fell from heaven at Pentecost and the Holy Spirit moved into his new house: the church. He is building a new Jerusalem, and we are that new Jerusalem. But this city will not ultimately compromise or capitulate to evil. One day it will have no spot or wrinkle. It will be a beautiful bride for the Lamb.

This chapter will not end in tragedy the way Nehemiah’s story does. This time the faithfulness of God will overwhelm and overthrow the faithlessness of man. This time the story will not end with compromising intermarriage with God’s enemies; it will end at the marriage supper of the Lamb.

We are living in a new chapter of the same story, in which God is still building a temple and a city for his people. My goal this morning is to make sure we all know what we are building and what we are fighting for.

In Nehemiah 4 the men knew exactly what they were building and fighting toward. We need to know too. I want to make sure we are all working from the same blueprints by reminding us of what we have already said. All the way back in July 2024, our lead pastor Andy wrote out the mission and vision of our church. As elders we all agreed on the basic content immediately and then refined the wording together until we were united on how to articulate it. In this sermon I will not be saying anything new. I am simply trying to unpack that same mission, vision, and strategy again so that we can keep pressing after it in this new year. So this morning I am going to answer three questions:

  1. What are we building and fighting toward?
  2. How do I build?
  3. How do I fight?

What Are We Building and Fighting Toward?

Our church’s mission and vision is what we are building toward. It has four parts:

  1. Make disciples
  2. who glorify God
  3. in all of life
  4. for all of Stillwater.

Make Disciples

The first part of our mission comes from the Great Commission. In Matthew 28 Jesus meets his disciples on a mountain. He is preparing to leave them. These are his final words:

“All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.”

Jesus commanded us to make disciples. Because Jesus possesses all authority—“all authority in heaven and on earth”—we should make disciples. As we do, we need not fear and we should not let up, because he is with us always, to the end of the age.

Making disciples includes evangelism, which is assumed in the command to baptize. If we are making disciples, we will be making converts. People who did not know Jesus or that he is King will hear about him from us. They will turn from trying to rule themselves and confess that Christ is Lord.

But making disciples is not finished once they are baptized. It has only just begun. Making disciples means teaching them to obey all that Jesus commanded. So we are not done making a disciple of someone until they are perfectly obedient—which is to say, never.

Making disciples is not something we do only “out there.” It certainly includes evangelism, but it is also what we are doing as parents, friends, neighbors, and fellow church members. When you look someone in the eye and shake their hand, you have an opportunity to make disciples. When someone comes to your home for dinner, you have an opportunity to make disciples. When your child asks you a question, you have an opportunity to make disciples. In other words, “making disciples” is the mindset we bring to every relationship, because it is what Jesus commanded.

Who Glorify God

The second part of our mission and vision is making disciples who glorify God. When you boil down what it means to obey all that Jesus commanded, it is this: glorify God. The chief end of man is to glorify God and enjoy him forever. So it is crucial that we understand how we do this.

Glorifying God has two crucial aspects. One happens in the heart and mind; the other happens with the body.

First, we glorify God by believing in him, by having faith—especially in his Son Jesus. We confess that Jesus is Lord and believe in our hearts that God raised him from the dead (Rom. 10:9), so that right now he possesses all power and authority. We love him, praise him, and depend on him. Ephesians 1:3–14 lays out our entire salvation. We are chosen by God, predestined, adopted, redeemed, forgiven, given an inheritance with Christ, and sealed by the Holy Spirit—all to the praise of his glorious grace. Every spiritual blessing we experience, we experience to God’s glory. That faith sinks down deep and changes us from the inside out. And as it changes us from the inside out, we begin to act differently.

That is the second part of glorifying God: obedience. Jesus tells us that if we love him we will keep his commandments (John 14:15). Paul tells us in Philippians 1:11 that when we are filled with the fruit of righteousness we glorify God. We get to work out our salvation because we have been born again and God continues to work in us for his good pleasure. This obedience is a crucial part of glorifying God. It is not some automatic add-on. It is so crucial that James says faith without works is dead faith—that is, not really faith at all.

Let me try to give you an analogy for how important obedience is. When God’s glory appears physically in the Bible it is typically with imagery of bright light, clouds, and fire. Glory is supposed to shine out, light up, flash out, fill up. Faith without works is like God filling the temple without smoke, or a burning bush without flame, or a pillar of fire without light. It is a contradiction.

So we want to make and be disciples who are born-again Christ-confessors who let their good works shine before men so that they glorify their Father in heaven (Matt. 5:16).

In All of Life

The third part of our mission is where we want to glorify God: in all of life.

We want to make disciples who glorify God in all of life. This is a way of saying that glorifying God is not a part-time job. It is not something we do in the morning when we read our Bibles and on Sundays when we are at church. It is what we are about everywhere. Anything else would be part-time obedience, which is not what Jesus meant when he told us to take up our cross and follow him.

We are here to make and be disciples who love and obey God in all of life—in our personal lives, in our families, at our work, in our church, and in the world as citizens. God tells us in Romans 12:1 that we are living sacrifices. Doug Wilson has commented somewhere that if we are living sacrifices, that means everywhere you go you are on an altar. As you sit at the breakfast table, your chair is an altar and you are the sacrifice. When you get to work, your desk is your altar. Children, as you sit and play together, the floor is your altar where you offer up a playful sacrifice of joy and contentment. Students, your desk at school is your altar. Wherever you go, you serve God there. Wherever you go, you were bought with a price and are called to glorify God with your body there (1 Cor. 6:20).

This means that we live coram deo, before the face of God, always. Our conviction must be that we live always before the face of God, everlastingly in the holy of holies with his Spirit inside us, his presence surrounding us, and his watchful eye upon us.

For All of Stillwater

And when we are faithful to God and walk according to his commands, he promises to bless our efforts. We envision the Stillwater area covered with the glory of God as the waters cover the sea—full of households whose marriages display Christ’s love for the church and the church’s respect for Christ; schools that help parents give their children a Christian enculturation; children brought up to learn God’s righteous paths as normal and desirable instead of constantly battling against the wicked culture of our age; God’s people doing good works in business and politics and real estate and restaurants and wedding venues and coffee shops and bookstores and bicycle rentals and breweries and school boards and county commissions and farming and financial services and manufacturing and museums; churches turned back to the true gospel; neighborhoods evangelized; businesses flourishing; and the justice system doing righteousness.

This is what Ezra and Nehemiah were laboring for. They did not just want a temple; they wanted a society with the temple at the center. They did not just want walls; they wanted a society that kept evil influence outside. This is the kind of Christian civilization that our forefathers risked their lives and died to create for us, and that is still worth building and fighting and dying for today.

It is why our church has an outward focus. Local churches are not supposed to be cul-de-sacs of God’s grace; they are supposed to be conduits. They are not supposed to be compounds but launch pads. We gather each week because God calls us. We leave because he commissions us to go and do this work.

That society will not be born here overnight, but it is what we dream about at night. It is what many of us talked about together as we looked over the city from Pioneer Park on Wednesday evenings this summer. It is what we pray for every time we recite the Lord’s Prayer: “Your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.”

In 1843 Stillwater was founded as the first city in Minnesota. The earliest settlers came to tame the wilderness and make life in this beautiful land of ten thousand lakes. Since then we have lost our way. Don’t you want to be part of redeeming this broken and sinful place? Wouldn’t it be great if God wrote a story in which our efforts here in the birthplace of Minnesota helped save Minnesota? I can imagine a hundred years from now our great-grandchildren smiling and shaking their heads when they find our names on another piece of strategic real estate, reap the benefits of our business partnerships, read our books, live in our houses, invest our money, sing the Psalms they learned from us, and cook our recipes.

How Do I Build?

That is the mission and vision. Our strategy is to “build and fight,” inspired by Nehemiah 4. When God’s people rebuilt the Jerusalem walls, “each of the builders had his sword strapped at his side while he built” (Neh. 4:18). Sword and trowel. Build and fight.

The reason we must do both is because we live among the ruins of what sin has destroyed, and God’s enemies—just like in Nehemiah’s day—want to make sure those walls are never built up again. So we have the challenge of rebuilding personal lives, households, and a society broken and marred by sin, and we have the challenge of fighting off those who want to keep it broken because they hate God and his ways. We need to be both craftsmen and warriors.

So first let’s talk about being craftsmen and answer our second question: “How do I build?” How do we build toward making disciples who glorify God in all of life for all of Stillwater?

Nehemiah is a great example to us.1

  1. First, he set an intention. When he heard that progress in Jerusalem had been set back, he formed an intention. He had a specific desire: “I want to set the return to the land up for success by getting a wall around the capital so that we can be a distinct people.” If you do not know what you are building or what you are fighting for, you are probably not doing much building or fighting.
  2. Then he made a plan. He knew what materials he would need and how long it would take. How would he get from cupbearer in Susa to overseeing wall-building in Jerusalem? He made a plan to make that happen.
  3. He prayed for God’s blessing. He knew that unless the Lord builds the house, the builders labor in vain.
  4. And then he acted courageously. He got started. He stuck his neck out and got to work.

That is how you build:

  1. Set an intention.
  2. Make a plan.
  3. Pray for God’s help.
  4. Get to work.

Now, we do that in five different spheres of life:

  • Personal
  • Household (fathers, mothers, children)
  • Vocation (nurse, plumber, doctor, etc.)
  • Church
  • Citizen

So we will look at each one and how you would build toward our mission and vision in that area.

Personal

You are you wherever you go. You are the same person at home, at work, and at church. You cannot keep them separate. So when you build a sound, healthy, faithful personal life, you are becoming the kind of person who can make disciples in all of life because you are one. We need to tend our hearts so that, with God’s blessing, they are full of life, brimming with God’s word, trusting in his promises, and relying on his help. So when you cultivate the ordinary means of grace to build up your faith—Bible reading, prayer, meditation—you are building toward our mission and vision.

Looking at how Jesus grew up gives us a good example for how we can build our personal lives. Luke 2:52 says that Jesus grew in wisdom and stature and in favor with God and man. One area of personal growth is internal: wisdom and knowledge. Another is external: stature and strength. Another is in favor with God, meaning increasing obedience. And another is in favor with man: learning relational wisdom and gaining gravitas.

To build up your personal life, you might start with an intention: I want to increase my wisdom about how to deal with those I disagree with. Then make a plan: I will survey Proverbs for advice and look at the Gospels to see how Jesus interacted with the Pharisees. Set a repeating event on your calendar so that you know when and where you will do it. Then pray for God’s help, and get started.

We are building when we make every effort to live sound, healthy personal lives.

Household

The second area we all live in is the household. Some of you are adults and still single. You are looking forward to beginning a household like the one I will describe. Others of us are in it now. To get a good idea of the kind of households we want to build, you should go back and listen to Andy’s series on God’s Good Design on manhood and womanhood.

Depending on who you are, you are aiming to build something different.

  1. Husbands, God has given us the responsibility to lead our households. We are building a faithful and fruitful marriage and faithful and fruitful children. Having responsibility means that we are responsible for results, not just effort. It is not our job to try to have a faithful and fruitful household. We emulate Jesus’s love. And Jesus did not just try to make the church his loving bride. His love and sacrifice transformed her. Our wives and children are like a garden that needs consistent water and sunlight. Our job is not merely to make sure they survive. Our job is to ensure that the woman God gifted us with flourishes and grows and is purified and beautified, just as Christ does for the church. How do we do this? By nourishing and cherishing. Men, there is no such thing as “set it and forget it” for our households. Just as you cannot water a plant and then leave it for a month, you cannot love your family really hard one weekend and then take the next few weeks off. We build up our households by consistent, sacrificial love.
  2. Wives, Ephesians 5 tells you to build up your household by submitting to your husbands as to the Lord. As the church submits to Christ, so also wives should submit to their husbands in everything. There is nothing that puts wind in the sails and fire in the bones of a man more than the love of his wife. As his helper, you glorify everything he does. An excellent wife is magical. She takes his raw efforts and transfigures them—turning his seed into giggling children, his house into a home he cannot wait to come back to, his table into family dinners, his grocery money into bacon for breakfast and chicken with focaccia for dinner, and his Christmas budget into gifts under the tree (that he may or may not see for the first time on Christmas). You build up your household…
  3. Spouses: A faithful and fruitful household starts with a faithful and fruitful marriage. If you have not read Song of Songs for a while, I would encourage you to give it another read. It is not sappy romanticism. It is healthy, passionate, you might say “head over heels” love between husband and wife. Cultivate a marriage like that—a marriage where your children do not just know that Mom and Dad are on the same team. Do not let them eulogize you by thinking, “They stayed together, so obviously they loved each other.” Show them the picture of marriage you hope they will have: a marriage that is loving, respectful, fun, sweet, and affectionate—a dance-in-the-kitchen, snuggle-on-the-couch, chat-in-the-car, laugh-at-the-table, bring-home-flowers-just-because kind of marriage.
  4. Grandparents, when your children move out you begin a new phase of life, but your influence does not go away. You still have a thousand ways to advise and encourage your children and to build up your grandchildren. I still remember that my Grandma Nancy and Grandpa Fred were at just about every baseball game I played. They showed me by their presence that they loved me and wanted to see me. And that sense of a family tribe made for a secure, happy childhood.
  5. Children, you build up the house you are in by honoring your parents. Young children, you should especially focus on doing this by obeying right away, all the way, with a happy heart. Older children, as you have grown up your parents give you fewer commands, but you can keep an honoring attitude toward them. Do not consider them fun-haters or fuddy-duddies who just do not get it. Honor them by guarding your heart and keeping it soft toward them.

We are building when we cultivate faithful and fruitful families.

Vocation

The third area of our lives is our vocation, or the job we get to do all day. If you are a student, this is school. If you are a stay-at-home mom, this is managing your household. Men, this is the job God has given you.

Ephesians 2:10 tells us that we were created in Christ Jesus for good works. The lion’s share of those good works happen in our vocation. You are a living sacrifice everywhere you go. That is why we should do our work with diligence and excellence. Strive to be outstanding at what you do because you are working as unto the Lord and not merely for men (Col. 3:23–24). That is how you build in your vocation: by doing your work as unto the Lord.

Our vocation is the way we sustain ourselves so that we can provide for our households and be generous with others. But that is not all it is. It is not just a means of money-making. It is where we invest our time, talent, and energy to build and fight for God. When you think about your vocation, you should not think of it merely as the way you convert your hours into dollars. Think about what you are actually creating. What is the result of your work? Does God want that result? Does it make the contribution you want it to make? God’s command to Adam to take dominion was taken up by the Second Adam—Christ. And we are his body. Our jobs are a major part of how Jesus takes dominion.

If you are in a job that you do not think is especially meaningful right now, one of the good works God might have for you is to chart and walk a path into work that you believe is more meaningful. And if so, work at it with all your heart. If, in God’s wisdom, now is not the time for that, then keep working as unto the Lord.

Some of you are working in jobs that are not your favorite. They are not what you want to be doing forever. If that is where God has placed you right now, that is okay. I spent years stocking shelves starting at 4:00 a.m. and more years as a bank teller. We have got to put food on the table and pay the bills. One of the good works God prepared beforehand for me was tens of thousands of pizzas placed on freezer shelves in Target and Walmart. I was never super passionate about frozen pizza or the banking industry. I was not trying to build a business in the industry. I was saving money for a wedding and for Maddie and me to start our life together. I spent years as a bank teller (and let me tell you, not many people came into that bank branch). Again, it was the best way I could find to pay rent. If that is the kind of job you are in, you can still do it unto the Lord. Working toward our mission means working with diligence and excellence. Stock the shelves with all the pizzas facing the right way, stack your pallet nicely back in the freezer, do not make a mess in the aisle. Remember that in Nehemiah the goldsmiths and perfumers—high and impressive jobs—did the good work of wall construction along with everyone else.

We are building when we provide for our households and take dominion for God.

Church Member

The fourth area we live in is the church. Ephesians 4:11–12 tells us that Jesus gave the church pastors in order to equip the saints for the work of the ministry so that the body of Christ would be built up. In fact, he says in Ephesians 4:16 that when the body is working properly, it builds itself up in love.

Remember that the church is a people, not programs. So being a part of God’s vision for the church is really simple: encourage one another and build each other up. Speak the truth in love. Use your words and actions to give one another courage to act, wisdom for discernment, comfort in affliction, a needed kick in the pants. Send soup when you hear someone is sick. Get on the phone when you hear someone is struggling.

Our best tool for being the kind of church that builds itself up in love is hospitality. Invite people over and make time to visit one another as often as you can. If you have not been invited to someone’s home, invite them. If they say no, do not be discouraged (how many times have you been too busy to say yes?). Just ask someone else.

We are building when we are encouraging our fellow church members.

Citizen

Finally, you are a citizen. This starts by being a good neighbor. You smile when you see your neighbors. You have them over for dinner. You host a summer barbecue. You bring a chainsaw when their tree falls and get to work. You share the gospel with them and let them see what a Christian household looks like.

Being a good citizen means paying some attention to what is happening in the world, both locally, nationally, and globally. At the very least you will pray about what you hear. You may be able to do something about it. You will certainly get the chance to vote. Being a good citizen includes doing your research and voting for the candidates who will advance righteousness the most.

Of course one great way to build in the area of citizen is to run for office—whether for city or county positions, school board, or state legislature. If you cannot run, you can support those who are (and by the way, Tom Dippel is running for an MN Senate seat right now).

We are building when we advocate for God’s paths of righteousness in all of life.

How Do I Fight?

Enemies Without

The main temptation to avoid in this realm as a citizen is being pressured by “the world” or “the culture” to back down or to act like their version of reality is normal—because it is not. Doug Rigney has called the pressure of the world the “progressive gaze.” In the days of Nehemiah he might have called this the “Sanballat gaze.” We are tempted to back down when we see rich and powerful enemies getting worked up about what we are doing. We are tempted to apologize, wring our hands, and get out of the way instead of boldly continuing to build and fight. Do not fall prey to backing down in order to appease your enemies. In Rules for Reformers, Wilson reminds us that we are in a war that has already been won. So there is no reason to make compromises with the enemy.

My wife was a great example of this earlier this year in a small way. She brought our kids to the library near our house. When we go to the library, our children need to bring us books they would like to look at before they look through them because, if you have not noticed, there are some pretty atrocious children’s books at the library these days. Well, one of my daughters found a book she liked, and instead of taking it quietly to Mama for approval, she yelled across the room, “Mama, is this book full of sin and lies?!” And instead of being embarrassed and making my daughter feel like she had done something wrong, she simply went over to take a look at it. She did not let the ire of the local public librarian make her nervous.

Do that on a small scale and a large scale. Live in God’s world without softening it for those who are pretending they can remake the world according to their liking.

We are doing our mission when we live upright, godly lives, trying to see God’s will done in our county and state and nation.

Enemies Within

I hope that the vision of our church and how we are all individually a part of it is inspiring to you. I hope it seems obvious and right and attractive in your eyes. So now is a good time to remember that we will face opposition. We will of course face opposition from the world, from the modern-day Sanballats and Tobiahs. But the hardest opposition to face will be the opposition from within.

Nehemiah 4:12 says that the Jews who lived nearby kept coming, over and over, telling the builders they needed to abandon Jerusalem and return to them. If this has not happened to you yet, it will. Your passion to see this vision happen—to make disciples who glorify God in all of life for all of Stillwater, to live holy and upright in every area of life—will draw some people who you thought were on your side: your friends and family, the people you are counting on to support you and have your back and encourage you. It is hard enough to deal with the disapproving gaze of the outside world. But that is at least expected. Of course God’s enemies will oppose us when we condemn wokeness leaking into Christian circles, or government schools, or voting for someone who encourages abortion. But when the people you thought were on your side line up against you and start to pressure you to back down, that hurts more.

It means that we need to be careful not to give in to appease our enemies or to give in to appease our friends. It is one of the reasons we emphasize sound biblical teaching so much here. We need to be sound and solid, with roots deep in the solid foundation of God’s Word, so that we can say, in Luther’s famous words, “Here I stand. I can do no other, so help me God.”

Conclusion

Allow me to summarize as we come to a close.

  1. What is the mission and vision of our church? To make disciples who glorify God in all of life for all of Stillwater.
  2. What does that mission and vision look like in our individual lives? Living a godly personal life, creating a faithful and fruitful household, working in a productive vocation, building up the church, and advocating for righteousness as a citizen.
  3. What should I build and fight first?
  4. Older men, build sound, exemplary lives.
  5. Older women, build young women who love their husbands and children.
  6. Young women, do your part to build a faithful and fruitful household.
  7. Young men, build self-controlled, upright, godly lives.
  8. How do I build and fight?
  9. Set an intention.
  10. Make a plan.
  11. Pray for God’s help.
  12. Get to work.

Let us pray.
