Let’s pray.

So much of the time we spend in our lives revolves around relationships. We’re interacting with coworkers, with relatives, with our children, with our spouse, with our in-laws.

Have you ever noticed that the more time you invest in people, the more time you spend with them, the more complicated your arguments get with them?

It’s easy to have a disagreement with someone who you only see a couple times a year. That woke cousin who still wears a mask to family gatherings, you know you won’t see him until next Christmas. The wimpy brother-in-law who lets his wife make all the decisions, well, he lives 7 states away so he’s just not in your day-to-day thinking.

But what about your wife? Your teenage sons and daughters? Your roommate?

You live with them. You see them every day and you’ve all had plenty of time to learn about what pushes each other’s buttons. You know exactly how to cut each other and how to provoke anger in one another, don’t you?

I want to use this opportunity to show you three facets of what we can call the “relationship diamond.”

I’m using the term “relationship diamond” because relationships are beautiful, and relationships are valuable…and relationships are very hard.

Today we’ll look at just three facets of this diamond.

First, I want to show you briefly how you’ve been influenced in how you think about relationships by post-Christian humanism…and how this is not a new thing.

Then, I want you to see God’s good design for how you can grow in your relationships.

Finally, and we’ll spend the most time here, I will give you some practical advice for how to help others change in their lives and in their relationships.

Let’s dive in.

I’ve got some bad news for you: you’ve been swimming in diseased water when it comes to how you think about relationships. Our culture, and every culture that has ever been, is concerned with being reborn.

We humans, in whatever religion we believe, and we all have a religion, don’t we? As we grow up, we all see that our major requirement in life is the need to start something new. We want to break free from the bondage of our past, or our parents, or the pressures we face in life, or the pain we’ve suffered.

We’ve all recognized, both Christians and pagans alike, that a definite problem exists.

The real question we need to be asking ourselves is, what is the problem?

When you read pop-psychology articles and books, and I’m not just talking about straight-up secular material, I’m talking about what you hear in mainstream evangelicalism.

When you read what’s being said about where our relationship problems come from, you are reading material that is antithetical to Scripture. You’re reading man’s failed attempt to integrate pagan, post-Christian, humanistic philosophy into the Church of the living God.

You’re reading things that are attempting to fuse, or meld together, two elements that cannot be fused and melded. God’s word is incompatible with humanist philosophy.

Here’s one example.

In a recent podcast series with associated articles on Christianity Today’s website, Christians are being taught the following concepts:

1. Parental success shouldn’t be measured by obedience, but by a parent’s own principles and self-awareness. 2. Your anxiety and emotional distress has been handed down to you by your parents or by your spouse. The cure is being able to self-differentiate. 3. Finally, your problems are rooted in your prejudice. One article claims that if you study what social psychologists agree on, you’ll see that the imagined, implied, or actual presence of others affects our thoughts and behaviors. The solution to your problems, in others words, is to empathize and affirm every culture you come into contact with.

I could go on and on, but I think you get the gist.

The massive problem with all of these theories is that man is at the center.

When we really think about how the Bible talks about our real problem, that is, our main problem, it talks about ultimate death. Ultimate and infinite eternity without God’s presence. That’s the greatest problem. And Christ has solved that problem. He took our problem and made it his problem. Then he solved it for those who would bow their knee to him.

If this is true, and it is, then what you consider to be your problems is actually quite revealing about you, isn’t it?

If you say your wife or your kids are your problem, then you are assuming that you can change them, or mold them.

Your wife and your kids may be a burden to you, and that may be true. And that is right for you to say. You can say that people are a burden to you.

So much of your relational friction, and your marital friction, is rooted in the fact that you think your wife is a problem to be solved. You think your son and your daughter and your husband are problems that you need to solve.

This is really just humanist thinking though, isn’t it?

If we could just change the environment, things would be so much better.

If we could just get a new job or a new house, we could be so much more content.

If I could just figure out a way to change my wife or my son, then I might finally find peace and joy.

And this really isn’t anything new under the sun, is it?

Do you remember what Nicodemus said?

He said, “How can a man be born when he is old? He cannot enter a second time into his mother’s womb and be born, can he?” (Jn 3:4).

Nicodemus couldn’t get his head wrapped around the possibility that man could actually be reborn in his mind and in his spirit without some form of control from man.

He believed rebirth was possible, just not the way Jesus taught it.

Instead, Nicodemus says, How can this be?? (Jn 3:9).

God is not just a representation of an ideal, brothers and sisters. It’s not as if God is “out there” and our world is the only reality.

If that were true, then every change in the world would need to come from man. If man needed to be reborn, then it will have to be a man-made project.

Do you see? Humanism proclaims that our problems are ours to be solved.

If you think people and circumstances in your life are your problem, and if you think that you are the one who needs to solve them, then you are proclaiming your own sovereignty. You are rejecting God’s sovereignty.

If you believe that only God can make man new, then God is sovereign.

What is aggravating your life is not what you perceive as your problems, it is that your man-made solutions have failed you.

I hope you can see how that first facet is foundational for what we are learning today.

It is so important to recognize the root cause of your problems, and to see that people and situations and circumstances are out of your control.

Now that we know the cause of our problems, you might be wondering to yourself, “you guys said this was a marriage and parenting conference, are you going to talk about how I can grow in my marriage and parenting?”

I’m glad you asked.

Perhaps the most clear, step-by-step process that has ever been uttered for the process of change in the Christian life was written by Paul.

Paul writes to Timothy, his young protege, who would carry the torch after he was gone. He wants him to know what to hand down to the church. He wants Timothy to know what type of foundation he and his followers will be able to stand on no matter what difficulties they might face.

He exhorts Timothy in this way, he says, “you must remain faithful to the things you have been taught. You know they are true, for you know you can trust those who taught you. You have been taught the holy Scriptures from childhood, and they have given you the wisdom to receive the salvation that comes by trusting in Christ Jesus. All Scripture is inspired by God and is useful to teach us what is true and to make us realize what is wrong in our lives. It corrects us when we are wrong and teaches us to do what is right. God uses it to prepare and equip his people to do every good work (2 Tim 3:14-17).

That’s not usually the translation I read from, but the NLT paints a clear picture of the steps involved for how we grow in our maturity, and by extension, how we grow in our relationships.

Paul taught this to Timothy. Now you can teach it to your wife and children and to those under your care.

Remember there’s nothing new under the sun…just like in the first century, there’s a lot of pressure in the world to focus on man-centered philosophies.

God says not to do that. God says that if we cling to His words, we will remain true until the end.

If you want to learn how to change, focus on the word. The word is adequate for every single thing we need to change and to help people change. After all, “His divine power has given us everything we need for a godly life” (1Pt 1:3).

Here’s the steps. There are four of them. And I think it’s wise to follow them in the order laid out in the Bible.

Step One. Teaching.

Teach God’s standards which relate to the problem you are facing. Are you helping someone with obedience problems? Do you need to grow in your practice of abstaining from sexual immorality? Does God have standards for these things?

Yes, he does. Study your Bible enough so that you can teach it to others. If you cannot identify God’s standards for an issue, then you won’t be able to teach the biblical principles for why an issue is important.

If you don’t know how to teach, ask a seasoned Christian or a pastor to help you so that you can become a better teacher. Let the body help you grow, so that you can help others grow!

Step Two. Conviction.

Now you’ll start to see why the order is important.

If you don’t teach about biblical principles for how to solve problems God’s way, then no one will be convicted about their problems!

Conviction follows awareness.

Now, let’s keep in mind that you are NOT a junior member of the Holy Spirit, that is, you cannot actually convict someone’s heart, but you can help to bring them to a knowledge of the truth.

You can show someone their sin and you can ask them to repent.

You can bring their sin into the light and ask them to do something about it.

For example, when your friend says, “look, you don’t understand. My problem is different. My problem is that when I get overwhelmed with desire, it just takes me over…I don’t want to do it, but I can’t control it.”

When this happens, you can lean on God’s word for help. You can say, “I see that this is a hard battle for you, but it’s not a unique problem for fallen men. You’ve just developed such strong habits in this area, that it’s become natural for you to sin in this way. After all, 1 Corinthians 10:13, says “No temptation has overtaken you that is not common to man. God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond your ability, but with the temptation he will also provide the way of escape, that you may be able to endure it.”

If you would like help in growing in your ability to use the Scripture in this way, please reach out to me afterwards and I would be happy to give you some excellent resources (p. 126, Adams).

Step 3. Correction.

Correction means to set straight something that’s been made crooked. In the Bible, it’s like guiding a ship to the right track because it’s been moved off-course.

When you study secular therapy, you’ll never see the words “conviction” or “repentance.” Believe me, I studied psychology for 4 years and I never heard those words!

Correction and repentance go hand-in-hand. When we want to see change in our lives and in the lives of others, we need to come to a completely different viewpoint of our attitudes, our actions, and our motivations.

If you want to correct the course, your first step is confession. Confession isn’t just some “feel good” exercise, it is a recognition that God is right and you were wrong.

Proverbs 28:13 says, “He who conceals his transgressions will not prosper/but he who confesses and forsakes them will find compassion.”

If you don’t confess, reconciliation and growth will not be possible.

Step 4. Training in righteousness.

Many of you in this crowd rejoice in, and seek to practice this concept already.

This is the same thing Paul is commanding fathers to do in Ephesians 6:4. He tells us to bring up our children in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.

Changing and helping others change involves compassionate listening, as well as a firm use of the rod.

Training those under our care “in righteousness” requires a lot of labor. Think of it like training for an athletic competition. Just like in martial arts, you need to discipline your mind and your body so that when a tournament comes, you have an arsenal of offensive and defensive tactics that will help you to win.

In this case, the goal isn’t just to win the fight.

It’s to grow in righteousness.

It’s remembering that we need to train new habits. We need to develop new habits because our old habits are killing us.

When someone comes to me and they say, “I’ve dealt with this problem for a long time and nothing works,” I always find out that they’ve been in the same rut for a long time. They haven’t actually changed their lifestyle. They haven’t done anything different.

My favorite way to say it is this, “If you always do what you always did, you’re always gonna get what you always got.”

In my life and in the lives of others, that has remained true.

So there we have it, the Bible gives us a four-step plan for what it looks like to change.

Now I’d like to get really practical with you, so that you can leave here with some simple tools and concepts for what you can do tonight, or this weekend, or when you get back home so that you can change and so that you can help others change.

The first thing you need to realize is that there are only three ways people respond to situations and circumstances. Once you think about this for a little while, and as you think about the situations and circumstances in your life, I hope you’ll see that this is true.

3 HUMAN RESPONSES

We respond to all situations and circumstances in our life in three ways, or three responses.

Neutral responses, loving responses, and unloving responses.

Neutral responses are those reactions that are normal to everyday life. These are normal expressions in your life that God does not hold against you as wrong.

For example, you are shocked and scared when someone jumps out from behind a door or startles you.

Another example is when you find joy and satisfaction in doing something well, like making a sale or getting a good grade.

Or when you hear some news, say, about the death of a loved one, and you are grieving and sad. Those are neutral responses.

One thing you must remember about neutral responses, however, is that they never stay neutral. After the initial response, these neutral responses will always become either unloving responses or loving responses. It can only go one of those two ways.

After something happens to us or someone says something to us, we either choose to respond by moving toward God, and we decide to pursue loving others and loving God, or we make the choice to move away from God. We choose unloving responses to people and circumstances.

If you don’t believe this, or the one you are trying to help does not believe this, then no type of truly biblical counsel that you offer them will help them. If they don’t recognize this principle, then you won’t be able to build on the next concepts I’m about to teach you.

Does anyone have any questions about this? Y’all tracking with me?

WHAT YOU CANNOT AND CAN CONTROL

The next biblical principle you need to understand, and this is critical for you and especially for those you are trying to teach, is the principle of what you cannot control and what you can control.

Think about this with me for a minute.

According to Scripture, we cannot control people or the outcome of situations.

Tell me, any of you, if you can think of a situation where this is not true. Anyone? Can we control others’ thoughts? Can we control others’ emotions? Can we control others’ desires? Others’ words? Others’ actions? Maybe…maybe we could tie them up…but we cannot control their will. Ecclesiastes 7:13-14 says,

“Consider the work of God: who can make straight what he has made crooked? In the day of prosperity be joyful, and in the day of adversity consider: God has made the one as well as the other, so that man may not find out anything that will be after him.”

God is sovereign over everything that happens in your life and in the life of those around you.

Since this is true, since we cannot control anyone else’s thoughts, emotions, desires, words, or actions… We can only control our own thoughts, emotions, desires, words, and actions.

Proverbs 16:32 says that he who rules his spirit is even stronger than a man who is mighty enough to conquer a city.

Paul says not to be conformed to this world, but to be transformed by the renewing of your what? Of your mind. He says that the more we practice this godly conformity, the more we’ll be able to discern good from evil. We’ll be growing straighter in the areas where we used to be crooked.

Therefore, if we want to change, we need to evaluate and take responsibility for how we are responding to people and the outcome of situations.

Whatever a man sows, that he shall also reap (Galatians 6:7-8).

We need to evaluate what is motivating us with people and the outcome of situations.

We need to find out the answer to the big question:

Are we motivated by love for God above our selfish desires? Or, are we motivated by our selfish desires above our love for God? Is everybody still with me?

Do you see why this is so important?

If you are trying to counsel someone who thinks that other people are the root cause of their problems, how much do you think they will grow?

I’ve counseled many individuals and couples who have said things like:

“If you had the boss I have, you’d be angry too!”

Or, “If you had the job or the car or the house I have, you’d be discontent too!”

Or the husband or wife who says, “yeah, I’m bitter and angry, if you lived with a wife or husband like this, you’d be bitter and angry too!”

But then when I walk them through this principle, and they see that the Bible teaches that other people don’t control their attitudes and emotions, they begin to see their own sin and how it is impacting their life and their relationships.

Do you see how giving counsel really does depend on whether or not these principles break through to a person?

If you or the one who you are counseling does not embrace this, then nothing else you tell them will help them.

BIBLICAL PROCESS FOR CHANGE

This leads me to one last practical help I want to offer you today.

Of course, I know this is a 30,000-foot overview, and we’re moving fast, so please reach out to me this weekend or shoot me an email after the conference as I’d love to help you flesh these principles out further.

The last principle that we’ll cover is the biblical process for change.

There are phases of change that we go through during the sanctification process, and knowing where the person is that you are trying to help… knowing where they are in the process will benefit you because you’ll be able to meet them and counsel them in the phase that they are in.

Your goal is to lead them into each successive phase, while remembering not to rush or to meet them in a phase they haven't reached yet.

As I have surveyed the Bible, and as I have been taught by wise teachers, I have come to believe there are 6 phases we follow as we put off our former man and seek to put on the new man through Christ (Eph 4:17-24). This pattern was first taught to me by a mighty man of God, Dr. Nicolas Ellen. And I will be forever grateful to him for teaching me so much about how to help my fellow brothers and sisters. These phases are:

Realization Remorse Renunciation Repentance Renewal Replacement

Bunch of R’s right?

I’ll walk through these briefly and give a Scriptural evidence for each, and then I’ll conclude for the day.

First, the Realization Phase. During this phase, the one you are ministering to comes to see the truth about their problem and he begins to see and understand how it applies to their life.

2 Timothy 2:24-27 says, “the Lord's servant must not be quarrelsome but kind to everyone, able to teach, patiently enduring evil, correcting his opponents with gentleness. God may perhaps grant them repentance leading to a knowledge of the truth, and they may come to their senses and escape from the snare of the devil, after being captured by him to do his will.”

This is very important.

If the person you are ministering to still thinks that people and circumstances are the root cause of their problem, they are not yet in the realization phase.

Do not pass GO. Do not collect $200!

If you give a person who is not here yet advice and application for godly living or some other doctrinal exercise to do, it will not help them.

They are not yet prepared to be broken over their sin, and therefore are not yet ready to change.

We’re trying to help them understand the ABC’s.

Do you know the ABC’s?

I’m talking about the overarching process here. An umbrella term for the whole thing. Awareness. Brokenness. Change.

That’s the only order you can go if you want to see true biblical maturation happen. Remember the ABC’s. Awareness. Brokenness. And Change.

Second step. The Remorse Phase.

In this phase, one comes to feel godly sorrow in relation to their sin and they will truly desire to make things right with God and others.

Paul was so encouraged wasn’t he, when he sent his letter to the Corinthians and then found out that they had repented? They were caused pain by Paul’s rebuke…even to the point of grieving. But this grieving ultimately led to genuine repentance. (2 Corinthians 7:10).

So many in the church today are afraid to talk about sin with one another. We’re afraid of ruffling feathers or not being “loving.”

Think of this step like a physician would before a surgery to remove a cancerous tumor. The surgeon knows that the patient will be in great pain after he cuts him open. But the pain is meant to be short-term, while the gain is meant to save his life.

And this is what we’re truly after, isn’t it? We want people to live and forget their old ways that were leading them into darkness.

The Bible says that “sorrow that is according to the will of God produces a repentance without regret, leading to salvation, but the sorrow of the world produces death.”

This sorrow isn’t just feeling bad because you got caught. It isn’t feeling bad because you got called out.

That’s just behavior modification. You can go pay the psychologist down the street $200 an hour if that’s all you want.

God is calling us to genuine repentance. A genuine recognition that you are wrong, and God is right.

If the person you are ministering to is not broken. They may not be a Christian. If you are stuck at this point, and you find that the person is not a Christian, you will be more efficient with your time by practicing evangelism instead of discipleship.

Thirdly, we seek to lead people into the Renunciation Phase.

James 5:16 says that when we’re open enough with one another to confess our sins, that is, to renounce and hate our sins like God hates them, then we will be strengthened to accomplish much for the Kingdom.

Does the person hate their sin? If they do, then it’s good and right to lead them on in the process.

After this, we should be teaching and leading the person into the Repentance Phase.

In this phase, you’ll see that genuine repentance isn’t just feeling bad that you got caught. I know I’ve already said this but it’s just so important.

This is an actual 180-degree turn in a new direction. A whole new way of living.

Proverbs 28:13 reveals that true and lasting heart change will not happen unless you confess your sin and then actually make a plan to forsake your sin.

God will be merciful to those who replace wicked habits with godly habits.

After repentance has occurred, and now we’re getting into the phases where you’ll need to be creative with the practical advice you give, you’ll guide your loved one into the Renewal Phase.

This is where you should study Ephesians 4:17-24 and be ready to teach it.

What areas of their business, their personal life, their actions, their friends, their habits, which areas are they still thinking like pagans think?

Paul is concerned in this passage with learning and teaching. What false teaching have you been listening to? Who are you learning from?

Is Jesus just an add-on to your life to make things a little sweeter?

The old person, the feeling-oriented person, needs to be replaced by the new man, who walks by faith and obedience to Jesus’ commands.

And finally, in the Replacement Phase, this is the final phase, you will see how the person will come to obey God and love others in the area where they had previously disobeyed God and been unloving towards others.

They will consistently be more reliable, honest, and hard-working.

You will see them seeking out ways to serve others and encourage others.

In other words, you’ll see good fruit replacing the bad fruit in their lives. They will be replanted near a stream that never runs dry.

So as you go out today and consider these things, be encouraged!

Know that you have been given all you need for life and godliness through God’s all-sufficient word.

He has gifted us with all we need to help one another find simple, practical, and lasting solutions to our problems.

He has made a way for us to help one another change, and has freely gifted us the ability to grow in our relationships, our marriages, and our faith.

Let’s pray.
